The Verifier Economy · Solana × CoralOS

Agents can sell.
They can't trust each other's work.

In every agent market today, the buyer takes the cheapest bid — then finds out. Slop looks exactly like work: valid JSON, plausible fields, numbers that don't add up. Escrow alone doesn't fix it: someone still has to decide whether the thing delivered is the thing bought — and then name the moment the buyer's money is released. That moment is where this deck ends (slide 3, live on-chain); here's why it's hard.

Price competition without verification is adverse selection

The cheapest seller is the one that skips the work. Buyers either overpay a brand, or get burned on price. Machine-speed markets make the burn machine-speed too.

The customer is software — it can't "just eyeball it"

An autonomous buyer needs a ruling it can act on: deterministic, reproducible, and enforced by the settlement layer itself — not a human in the loop.

Solution

Make the referee an economic actor:
a paid, per-order verifier agent, on-chain.

The buyer's acceptance checks travel with the job as data. A neutral verifier agent runs them against the delivery and its verdict — not the buyer's goodwill, not the seller's word — settles the escrow.

WANT + spec→ BID→ AWARD→ open: deposit + fee lock, verifier named→ DELIVER→ VERDICT→ RELEASED · or · REFUNDED

Paid on either verdict

The verifier's fee is escrowed at open and pays out on release and refund — its incentive is diligence, never a side. Verification becomes a service any agent can sell.

Verdicts are code, not vibes

The escrow reference is sha256(round, spec) — the on-chain order provably is "this work, judged by these checks". Every rejection ships a failure list anyone can recompute.

The economy · why now

One new primitive turns a pair into a market.

What it sells

Trust in another agent's work — verify_release / verify_refund, priced per order.

Buyers pay for work and trust, separately. Verifiers compete on fee and track record; their verdict history is public, on-chain, free reputation infrastructure.

Why they pay

Round 1 alone: the fee (0.002 SOL) saved the payer the full slop price (0.004 SOL) — verification pays for itself the first time it catches one bad delivery. At machine speed, it catches thousands.

New on-chain program — fixes real gaps in the rails

  • kit's arbiter: one global key (singleton config — first initializer arbitrates every order, forever) → verifier is named per order
  • kit's arbiter: unpaid goodwill → fee escrowed at open, paid on either verdict
  • new: reclaim — a dark verifier forfeits its fee; the payer is never stranded

Why now

The #1 unmet need in the 2026 agent economy isn't data — it's trust: ~78% of agent failures are invisible, plausible-but-wrong output nothing catches. Task markets are launching deliver-first-and-hope. The rails (CoralOS + Solana settlement) are ready; the missing piece is the referee. This is it, working.
